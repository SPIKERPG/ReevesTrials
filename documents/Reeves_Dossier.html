■ BIOGRAPHICAL SUMMARY

Dr. Elias James Reeves IV is a neurological researcher and behavioral scientist who came to the attention of the World Health Organization's Emergency Mental Health Committee in 2045. He was among the first cohort of researchers formally assessed by the committee following the establishment of the WHO Underground Mental Health Summit (WHOUMHS) in response to the global SPIKE crisis.

At the Summit's inaugural session, Dr. Reeves submitted a proposal for a broad-spectrum neurological intervention. The proposal was reviewed by the full committee and deprioritized in favor of a more targeted therapeutic approach. Dr. Reeves accepted this determination without formal objection and indicated his intention to pursue a revised research direction.

■ COMMITTEE NOTE — WHOUMHS SESSION 01 Initial proposal reviewed and declined. Concerns noted regarding scope and delivery methodology. Researcher advised to revise in line with committee parameters. No further record of the original proposal contents exists in public archive.

Following the deprioritization of his original proposal, Dr. Reeves pivoted to gene therapy research with a stated focus on neurological resilience in future generations — a natural continuation of his prior interests, adapted to committee-approved parameters. He was subsequently awarded a Division IV research commission in 2046 and assigned to Research Establishment 01 (RE-01) in the former state of Indiana, United States.

RE-01 operated under ongoing UGD monitoring. Facility records for the upper research levels were submitted as required. Dr. Reeves is noted to have managed a staff of personnel and to have maintained the facility's operational mandate through Year 0.

RE-01 ceased reporting at Year 0. Subsequent contact attempts yielded no response. Dr. Reeves did not report to UGD administrative intake in the post-Reset period. It is the working assessment of Division IV that he underwent the Reset alongside the general population. His post-Reset whereabouts are not confirmed.
