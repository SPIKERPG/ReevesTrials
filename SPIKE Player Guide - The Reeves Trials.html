SECTION 01 // OPERATIVE BRIEFING
THE WORLD YOU WERE BORN INTO

World War III did not end with a treaty. It ended with a silence. The convergence of conventional warfare, a nuclear exchange over the Black Sea, engineered pandemic, and complete societal breakdown claimed over three billion lives. What the war didn't kill, the aftermath threatened to finish.

The period following the Collapse became known as SPIKE, a global wave of psychological breakdown, violent crime, and societal self-destruction. Clinicians called it Emotional Sickness. Survivors called it the end of everything. Civilization was three decisions from extinction.

The restructured United Nations, now the United Global Directorate (UGD), commissioned emergency neurological research across four global facilities. One of them worked. At the beginning of Year 0, an intervention known as the Reset was implemented worldwide. SPIKE ended. Rebuilding began. The world has been at peace for five years.

YEAR 5 // THE WORLD TODAY

The UGD administers a stable global society. National borders have dissolved into regional administrative districts. A global communications network has just been restored. Food production, infrastructure, and basic services function across most of the inhabited world.

The world is orderly. It is functional. By every measurable standard, it is at peace. You serve that peace.

SPIKE // WHAT IT MEANS IN THE FIELD

SPIKE is the UGD's clinical term for deviation above neurological baseline. It is measured on a scale of 0 to 11. All UGD field operatives carry a subcutaneous SPIKE implant, issued at HQ, which reads your own level continuously and can evaluate others at close range, replacing the need for a handheld wand in the field. Wands remain in civilian use. A reading of 0 is normal. Elevated readings indicate increasing instability and must be managed.

SPIKE is a medical condition, not weakness. Operatives experiencing elevated readings are expected to report them, take countermeasures, and continue the mission.

You will encounter SPIKED individuals in the field. They are in acute distress. Prolonged proximity elevates your own readings. Limit contact. Document encounters. Report on return.

⚠ PRE-MISSION INCIDENT // THE SPIKE STORM — APPROXIMATELY ONE WEEK AGO

Approximately one week before this briefing, a SPIKE Storm swept the global population. Not a regional anomaly, a worldwide event. Rapid, involuntary SPIKE escalation across every UGD district simultaneously. Duration: several hours. Casualties confirmed. Origin unknown. The Storm dissipated without explanation, but it cannot be guaranteed not to recur.

For five years, the RE-01 file sat deprioritized. The Reset worked. Rebuilding took precedence. Dr. Reeves' research was classified as resolved — a footnote to a solved problem. The Storm changed that assessment within days. The UGD does not fully understand how the Reset functions, only that it did. The Storm proved that SPIKE can return. This commission was expedited. You are standing in this briefing room because of what happened one week ago.

Anticipate elevated ambient SPIKE risk in the field. The Storm may have raised baseline readings across the general population. Readings you encounter may not reflect individual trauma, they may reflect residual Storm exposure. Document everything. Your implant will flag anomalies automatically.

YOUR MISSION // OPERATION: REEVES TRIALS

You are UGD Operatives, commissioned by Division IV to investigate Research Establishment 01 (RE-01), located in what was formerly the state of Indiana, United States. RE-01 was identified in recovered archives as a site of early SPIKE-related neurological research — the nature and outcomes of which are, at present, classified or unknown.

PRIMARY OBJECTIVES
  • Locate and retrieve any surviving research documentation from RE-01
  • Assess the current state and security infrastructure of the facility
SECONDARY OBJECTIVES
  • Identify links between RE-01's research and other known global facilities
  • Document any anomalous phenomena or readings encountered on-site

Intelligence on RE-01 is limited. Conditions inside are unknown. Prioritize extraction over engagement. Keep your SPIKE readings low.

EMOTIONAL FLAWS

Emotional Flaws are scars, acquired through play when a character suffers extreme trauma. They're roleplay-first, shaping how your operative carries what they've been through. The flaws below are examples; the GM and player may define others.

A flaw is not a mechanical penalty with a name. It is a persistent lens through which the character processes stress — a pattern that formed under pressure and has not been dismantled. When a trigger situation arises, the character feels the pull of the flaw before they make any decision about it.

Flaws are not chosen at creation — they are acquired during play. When a flaw triggers, you must declare whether you are leaning in or resisting before any dice are rolled. You cannot wait to see the outcome first.

⚠ SESSION 1 RESTRICTION

Emotional Flaws are not assigned at character creation and do not apply in Session 1. They emerge through play when the GM determines that a character has experienced trauma significant enough to leave a mark. Flaw slots on your dossier begin empty.

LEAN INTO IT

Act according to the flaw's impulse — let it drive your character's behaviour in this moment. No roll required.

Gain +1 to the triggering roll OR +1 COMPOSURE (your choice). The flaw does not disappear — it will trigger again.

RESIST IT

Attempt to act against the flaw's impulse. Roll d10 + RESOLVE vs TN 9.

Success: act normally this instance — the flaw was held in check. Failure: −1 to the triggering roll OR +1 SPIKE (GM's choice based on context). Either way, the flaw remains.

Leaning in is not failure — it is characterisation. Resisting is valid, but it carries risk: the flaw is still there, and the RESOLVE roll is not a guarantee.

REDUCING A FLAW

Flaws can only be reduced through meaningful narrative resolution — not mechanical advancement. This requires multiple sessions of directly confronting what caused the flaw, and explicit GM approval. It is rare by design. A flaw is not a problem to solve. It is something your operative carries.

Fear of Attachment
Trigger: Forming or relying on close bonds. Effect: −1 to social rolls involving trust. The operative finds themselves pulling back from dependence before they realise they're doing it — deflecting warmth, keeping information from teammates, framing cooperation as logistics rather than connection. Lean: +1 when acting independently.
Bottled Rage
Trigger: Frustration, disrespect, loss of control. Effect: Risk of aggressive response. The flaw surfaces as a sudden sharpness — a clipped word, a threat that didn't need to be made, a physical action that was harder than necessary. It is not wild; it is controlled pressure looking for an exit. Lean: +1 to Close Combat.
Emotional Numbness
Trigger: High-stress or emotional situations. Effect: −1 to social connection rolls. The operative goes flat at the moments that demand something more. They can be present in body while registering nothing — answering correctly, completing tasks, and leaving other people with the feeling that no one came home. Lean: Immune to first COMPOSURE loss per scene.
Paranoia
Trigger: Uncertainty, hidden information. Effect: Difficulty trusting others. The operative reads ambiguity as threat. Motives that are unclear become motives that are hostile. They may be right more often than not, but the cost is paid in every interaction where they weren't. Lean: +1 to Perception checks.
Survivor's Guilt
Trigger: Others being harmed or at risk. Effect: Hesitation or self-sacrifice pressure. The operative struggles to act on their own behalf when others are in danger, even when action would serve both. The flaw does not feel like guilt — it feels like clarity, like the correct priority ranking, which is what makes it persistent. Lean: +1 when actively protecting others.
Control Fixation
Trigger: Chaotic or unpredictable situations. Effect: Must attempt to control outcomes. When circumstances move faster than the operative can organise them, the response is not adaptation but escalating effort to impose structure — more planning, more instruction, more insistence that things proceed in the right order. Lean: +1 to planning or preparation actions.

SECTION 04 // COMBAT OPERATIONS
TURN STRUCTURE
Each operative's turn consists of:
  • 1 Action — Attack, assist, use a skill, technical action, grounding attempt
  • 1 Minor Action — Move 1 zone, draw a weapon, quick interaction

Actions and Minor Actions may be used in any order.

INITIATIVE

All participants roll d10 + AWARENESS and act in descending order.

Ties: player preference or GM call. Initiative is fixed for the encounter.

ATTACK RESOLUTION — MARGIN OF SUCCESS

d10 + ATTRIBUTE + SKILL vs ENEMY DEFENSE VALUE

One roll determines both hit and damage. If your total meets or exceeds the enemy's Defense Value, the attack lands. The margin (your total minus their Defense) becomes your base damage.

BASE DAMAGE  =  ROLL TOTAL − DEFENSE VALUE
FINAL DAMAGE  =  BASE + WEAPON MODIFIER − ARMOR
WEAPON MODIFIERS
  • Light (pistol, knife): margin only
  • Standard (rifle): margin + 2
  • Heavy (shotgun, sniper): margin + 4  — cannot move and attack same turn. Shotgun: CLOSE/NEAR range only.
ARMOR
  • Light armor: −5 final damage, no penalty
  • Medium armor: −7 final damage, −1 Mobility
  • Heavy armor: −10 final damage, −1 Mobility, −1 AWARENESS. Extended movement costs +1 COMPOSURE.

SECTION 05 // THE SPIKE SCALE
WHAT IS SPIKE?

SPIKE is your operative's measure of neurological deviation from Reset baseline, 0 to 11. It rises in response to trauma, hostile environments, and specific field threats. It falls through rest and grounding.

SPIKE is pressure, not failure. An operative is measured by whether they manage it before it manages them.

LEVELDESIGNATIONMECHANICAL EFFECT
0 SERENE Baseline. No mechanical effect. The ideal operative state.
1–2 STABLE No mechanical effect. Nominal baseline.
3–5 SHAKEN −1 to all MIND rolls. SPIKE Saves (d20 + RESOLVE vs TN 9) when triggered.
6–7 CRACKED −1 to MIND and RESOLVE. Hallucinations possible. Saving Throw required when triggered.
8–9 OVERLOADED Disadvantage on all social checks. Operative unreasonable in roleplay. SPIKE Save required hourly.
10 SPIKED Loss of character control. Roll Behavior Table (d20) at turn start. End of turn: d20 + RESOLVE vs TN 16. Nat 20: SPIKE −2. Nat 1: SPIKE +1.
11 CONSUMED 1d4 True Damage per turn until HP 0, then 1d4 RESOLVE damage per turn. Death follows. End of turn: d20 + RESOLVE vs TN 18. Nat 1: damage at Advantage.
SPIKE SAVES — HOW THEY WORK

A SPIKE Save is always d20 + RESOLVE. It is not a skill check — RESOLVE is the only addition, and no skill bonus applies. The TN depends on your current band (see table above).

When a save is triggered. "When triggered" means a specific event is pushing your character toward further escalation: extreme trauma, a failed grounding attempt, proximity to a CONSUMED operative, or a direct SPIKE-inducing effect. The GM calls for the save at the moment of the trigger. At OVERLOADED (8–9), a save is also required once per hour of sustained elevated exposure even without a specific event. At SPIKED (10) and CONSUMED (11), the save is automatic — it happens at the end of every turn.

What success and failure mean. Success means you hold your current level — the pressure was real and you resisted it. Failure means SPIKE increases by 1. At SPIKED (10), two special results apply to the end-of-turn save: a natural 20 reduces SPIKE by 2, and a natural 1 raises it by 1 (risking CONSUMED). At CONSUMED (11), a natural 1 causes that turn's damage rolls to be made at Advantage.

COMPOSURE 0. Reaching zero COMPOSURE triggers an immediate SPIKE Save (d20 + RESOLVE vs TN 14) regardless of your current band. This is separate from any end-of-turn save — both apply if you are also SPIKED.

LOWERING SPIKE
  • Short Rest: SPIKE −1
  • Long Rest: SPIKE −2
  • Grounding Action: Once per encounter — Action + d20 + MIND vs TN 13. Success: SPIKE −1. An ally at SPIKE 4 or lower may grant Advantage.
ASSISTING A SPIKED OPERATIVE

Requires an Action and roleplay — logic, routine, calm command.

d20 + MIND vs TN 14

  • Success: Target gains Advantage on next SPIKE Save
  • Failure: Operative lashes out — 1d4 damage to helper
  • Critical Failure: Both make RESOLVE Save TN 12. Target has Disadvantage on next save.

SPIKE RESONANCE

When two individuals' SPIKE levels differ by 4 or more, the lower-SPIKE individual experiences a discomforting instability in proximity — described as "static under the skin." Both suffer Disadvantage on social checks while the gap persists. Log all resonance events upon mission return.

SECTION 06 // ADVANCEMENT
MILESTONE PROGRESSION

Advancement is milestone-based, signaled by the GM. Milestones are narrative beats: a new understanding, a meaningful decision, a point of no return. At each milestone, choose one:

Gain a New Skill
Add any untrained skill at Trained level (+2).
Specialize a Skill
Upgrade a Trained skill (+2) to Specialized (+3).
Gain a Trait
Select any trait from Section 02. Maximum 6 traits per operative.
Increase Max COMPOSURE
+1 to maximum COMPOSURE. Subject to GM-set campaign cap.
Reduce an Emotional Flaw
Rare. Requires meaningful narrative resolution across multiple sessions and GM approval.
WHAT ELSE GROWS

Statistics are one dimension. Operatives also progress through what they learn and who they know:

  • Knowledge: What you uncover stays with you. Information is sometimes the only resource that matters.
  • Access: Credentials, clearance codes, and bypassed security open doors that force cannot.
  • Equipment: Better tools recovered in the field or requisitioned from the Directorate.
  • Allies: Contacts who owe your team something may be worth more than any weapon.
